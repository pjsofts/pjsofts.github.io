The thread through all 4 hours

Three rules for the whole search

Every module comes back to these three. If a tactic breaks one of them, we drop it.

  1. 01

    Measure everything

    Applications, replies, screens, offers, by channel. If you aren’t tracking it, you’re guessing.

  2. 02

    Never list what you can’t defend

    Every line on your resume needs a story, a number and a link behind it. Use AI to sharpen what’s true, never to invent what isn’t.

  3. 03

    Distribution beats talent

    The best engineer nobody can find loses to a good one everybody can. Being visible is part of the job.

The four users

Your application gets read four times

First by you, then by software, then by a recruiter who gives it five seconds, then by engineers who will ask about every line. Most people only write for one of them. We write for all four, in order.

The agenda

5 modules, 24 topics

We start with where to aim, then build the evidence, make it visible, get through the interviews, and measure the whole thing so it improves every week.

01

Strategy

Stop applying everywhere

  1. Why volume is deadAuto-apply bots tripled applications per hire. Sending more now hurts more than it helps.
  2. The four usersYou, the ATS, HR and the dev team each score you differently. You have to pass all four, in order.
  3. Your problem statementTreat the search like a product: who is hiring, what they need, and why you.
  4. Picking one laneOne target role, one resume, one story. Spreading thin is how good people stay invisible.
  5. Grounding in market dataUse real demand and salary data to pick the lane, not guesswork or hype.
You leave with

One lane, written down, with the data behind it.

02

Evidence

Every line backed by proof

  1. The Brag DatabaseA running log of what you shipped, the numbers, and the links that prove it.
  2. The Friday setupFifteen minutes a week that turns merged PRs and wins into resume lines.
  3. How ATS reads youParsing, semantic matching and scoring. Why a nice-looking resume can come out blank.
  4. Lane resumesScore your own resume against the job description before the ATS does it for you.
  5. Defending every lineIf you can’t explain it for ten minutes in an interview, take it off.
You leave with

A Brag Database and a resume you have already scored.

03

Proof

Make your skills visible

  1. GitHub as proofSpecs, tests, evals, and the why written into your PRs. Reviewers read commits.
  2. The depth ladderFrom tutorial clone to production-shaped project, one rung at a time.
  3. Portfolio in 5 secondsOne page that shows your strongest skill before anyone scrolls.
  4. LinkedIn & distributionGetting found by recruiters, building in public, and posting without cringe.
  5. Referrals & channel mathWhich channels actually turn into interviews, and where to spend your hours.
You leave with

GitHub, portfolio and LinkedIn that tell the same story.

04

Interviews

From first screen to final round

  1. Company briefsUse AI to research the company and team before every call, grounded in real sources.
  2. Study guides & story tablesBuild prep material from the job description, plus your stories mapped to it.
  3. How AI screeners score youMore first rounds are scored by AI from a transcript now. Know what it looks for.
  4. Practising the AI screenRehearse with an AI interviewer and rubric before the real thing.
  5. Final roundsSystem design, live coding, and defending your resume in front of the team.
You leave with

A prep kit you can reuse for every company.

05

Iterate

Measure, learn, negotiate

  1. Funnel trackingApplications → screens → interviews → offers, by channel. Find where you drop.
  2. PostmortemsEvery rejection is data. A short, honest review after each one.
  3. Offers & negotiationA comparison matrix, a counter you can say out loud, and when to walk away.
  4. Running the systemAI agents as a resume skill, and the habits that keep the search going.
You leave with

A tracking sheet and a negotiation script.
